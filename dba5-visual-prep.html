Causes of WWII & U.S. Neutrality

Quick context
understand

Explain how the Treaty of Versailles, the rise of fascism, and appeasement each contributed to the start of WWII. Which do you think mattered most?

How to approach this:
analyze

The U.S. passed multiple Neutrality Acts but also supplied the Allies through Lend-Lease. At what point did neutrality become fiction? What finally pushed the U.S. into open war?

How to approach this:
evaluate

Was America's entry into WWII inevitable after Pearl Harbor, or could a different government have chosen to stay out? What would that have meant for the outcome?

The Home Front & the Holocaust

Quick context
understand

Describe one way the U.S. home front changed during WWII — for industry, for women, or for a specific group of Americans.

How to approach this:
analyze

The U.S. interned 120,000 Japanese Americans but not Italian or German Americans in significant numbers. What fears and assumptions explain that difference? Does the wartime context justify it?

How to approach this:
create

You are a government official in 1942. Intelligence suggests some members of a particular ancestry group may be disloyal, but most are loyal citizens. You can monitor everyone, restrict movement near bases, intern the group, or protect all rights fully. What do you recommend? What are you risking with each choice?

Ending the War & Its Consequences

Quick context
understand

Name the two atomic bomb targets and explain what happened in the days between the two bombings.

How to approach this:
analyze

Historians still debate whether dropping the atomic bombs was necessary to end the Pacific war or was partly a message to the Soviet Union. What evidence supports each interpretation, and why can't we settle the question definitively?

How to approach this:
evaluate

WWII produced the Nuremberg Trials, the United Nations, and the rise of two superpowers. Which consequence do you think most shaped the world that followed, and why?
