U.S. & the Middle East / Reagan Era

Quick contextYou are an American in 1980. The 1970s were hard: inflation, unemployment, an energy crisis, and growing entanglement in the Middle East, from oil embargoes to the hostage crisis in Iran. Ronald Reagan promises to fix it: lower taxes, cut government, rebuild military strength. Is he right? Will this work?
understand

Describe the Yom Kippur War, the Iran Hostage Crisis, and the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan. How did each one pull the U.S. deeper into Middle Eastern affairs?

How to approach this:
For each event, explain what happened and what American interest was at stake: oil, hostages, or Cold War strategy. Show how each one pulled the U.S. deeper into the region.
analyze

Reagan's economic policies (Reaganomics) promised growth through tax cuts and deregulation. Who benefited most from these policies, and who did not? What does that distribution reveal?

How to approach this:
Look at what happened to different groups: investors, high earners, factory workers, the poor. Who gained the most and who was left out? What does that pattern reveal?
evaluate

The Iran-Contra affair showed that the executive branch was conducting secret foreign policy that bypassed Congress. Does national security justify that kind of secrecy? What is the strongest argument on each side?

How to approach this:
State what the executive branch did and why it was hidden from Congress. Then weigh the case for secrecy in national security against the case for congressional oversight, and take a position.
EOC Practice Question

What was intended to result from Reaganomics policies?

Correct Answer: B

Why this is correct: Reaganomics, based on supply-side economic theory, cut income tax rates, reduced regulation on business, and slowed the growth of domestic spending. Supporters believed lower taxes would encourage investment and work, with benefits "trickling down" through the economy, while tighter monetary policy would tame the high inflation of the 1970s. Inflation did fall sharply in the early 1980s and the economy grew after 1983, though federal deficits and the national debt rose substantially.

Why the other answers are historically incorrect:
A: Reagan sought to slow domestic social spending, not expand it; his budgets shifted resources toward defense.

C: The era weakened organized labor; Reagan's firing of striking air traffic controllers in 1981 signaled a tougher federal stance toward unions.

D: Deregulation reduced government control of business in industries like airlines, trucking, and banking.

Terrorism, Human Rights & Nuclear Threats

Quick contextYou are an American in the years after September 11, 2001. Terrorism has reached U.S. soil. The government responds with new laws, new agencies, and wars abroad. Meanwhile, nuclear weapons spread to more nations. How does America stay safe without giving up what it stands for?
understand

Name one specific U.S. policy or action taken after September 11, 2001 and explain its stated purpose.

How to approach this:
Pick one specific response: the Patriot Act, the Department of Homeland Security, or the war in Afghanistan. Explain what problem it was created to solve.
analyze

After 9/11, the U.S. passed the Patriot Act, created the Department of Homeland Security, and opened Guantanamo Bay. Which of these responses do you think most strained civil liberties, and why?

How to approach this:
For each response, identify which liberty it affected: privacy, due process, or humane treatment. Compare them and defend which strain on civil liberties was most serious.
create

You are advising a president on nuclear proliferation. One country has secretly developed nuclear weapons. You can pursue diplomacy, economic sanctions, cyberattacks, or military action. Each has costs and risks. What do you recommend, and what principles guide your choice?

How to approach this:
Commit to one recommendation. Explain what outcome you expect, what could go wrong, and what principle guides your choice: security, diplomacy, or precedent.
EOC Practice Question

After September 11, 2001, which response to terrorism most strained civil liberties?

Correct Answer: B

Why this is correct: The Patriot Act authorized government agencies to conduct surveillance, monitor communications, and detain individuals suspected of terrorism with reduced traditional legal protections. This significantly expanded executive power and created tension between security measures and constitutional rights like privacy and due process. It remains controversial among civil liberties advocates.

Why the other answers are historically incorrect:
A: While airport screening increased, it is a reasonable security measure and does not significantly restrict civil liberties like the Patriot Act did.

C: Police training does not inherently strain civil liberties.

D: Passport requirements for international travel are a normal immigration procedure, not a strain on domestic civil liberties in the same way the Patriot Act was.

EOC Practice Question

Which of the following best describes major challenges America has faced regarding environmental, social, and health issues since 1990?

Correct Answer: B

Why this is correct: Since 1990, America has faced environmental crises (climate change, pollution), widening social inequality (income gaps), and health challenges (opioid epidemic, pandemic response, healthcare costs). Despite awareness and some government programs, progress has been slow and inconsistent. These remain pressing unsolved problems.

Why the other answers are historically incorrect:
A: Environmental regulations exist (EPA, Clean Air/Water Acts) but enforcement and effectiveness remain contested and insufficient.

C: Health disparities actually increased in many communities despite advances in medical technology available to the wealthy.

D: These challenges have not been solved; they remain defining issues facing America in the 21st century.
