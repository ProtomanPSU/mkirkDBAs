U.S. & the Middle East / Reagan Era

Quick context The 1970s oil crisis, the Iran Hostage Crisis, and the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan drew the U.S. deeper into Middle Eastern affairs. Reagan won the 1980 election on promises of tax cuts, deregulation, and Cold War resolve — policies that grew the economy and the deficit simultaneously while reshaping the Democratic-Republican divide.
understand

Describe the Yom Kippur War, the Iran Hostage Crisis, and the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan. How did each one pull the U.S. deeper into Middle Eastern affairs?

How to approach this: Cover all three with one concrete consequence each — oil embargo, hostage diplomacy, covert CIA support for the mujahideen. Then explain the cumulative pattern: what kind of entanglement did these events create?
analyze

Reagan's economic policies (Reaganomics) promised growth through tax cuts and deregulation. Who benefited most from these policies, and who did not? What does that distribution reveal?

How to approach this: Name specific groups on both sides — upper-income earners, financial sector versus industrial workers, rural communities. Then draw the larger conclusion: what economic theory was being tested, and what did the results show?
evaluate

The Iran-Contra affair showed that the executive branch was conducting secret foreign policy that bypassed Congress. Does national security justify that kind of secrecy? What is the strongest argument on each side?

How to approach this: Present both arguments genuinely. The strongest pro-secrecy argument isn't just 'national security' — it's about operational necessity and adversarial intelligence. The strongest anti-secrecy argument isn't just 'laws were broken' — it's about democratic accountability and precedent.

Terrorism, Human Rights & Nuclear Threats

Quick context The September 11 attacks triggered the PATRIOT Act, the Department of Homeland Security, the invasion of Afghanistan, and later Iraq. These decisions launched debates about civil liberties, torture, indefinite detention, and the limits of executive power that continued across multiple presidencies.
understand

Name one specific U.S. policy or action taken after September 11, 2001 and explain its stated purpose.

How to approach this: Pick one with enough substance to discuss — the PATRIOT Act (surveillance to detect terrorism), the invasion of Afghanistan (destroy al-Qaeda and Taliban), Guantanamo Bay (detain enemy combatants outside normal legal jurisdiction). Stated purpose is separate from whether it worked or was justified.
analyze

After 9/11, the U.S. passed the Patriot Act, created the Department of Homeland Security, and opened Guantanamo Bay. Which of these responses do you think most strained civil liberties, and why?

How to approach this: Pick one and explain the specific civil liberty it affected — Fourth Amendment searches, habeas corpus, free association. The strongest answer traces a specific legal or policy consequence, not just a general concern.
create

You are advising a president on nuclear proliferation. One country has secretly developed nuclear weapons. You can pursue diplomacy, economic sanctions, cyberattacks, or military action. Each has costs and risks. What do you recommend, and what principles guide your choice?

How to approach this: Name your recommendation and the principle behind it — deterrence, non-proliferation norms, proportionality. Then acknowledge the biggest risk of your chosen option and explain why it's still preferable to the alternatives.

Environmental, Social & Health Issues

Quick context Modern America has faced overlapping domestic crises: the HIV/AIDS epidemic, the opioid crisis, COVID-19, and climate change — each revealing gaps between public health needs and government capacity or political will. Globalization reshaped the economy, creating new wealth while displacing manufacturing workers and communities.
understand

Name one environmental or public health crisis from modern American history and describe how the government initially responded.

How to approach this: Pick one you know well enough to discuss the response specifically — the HIV/AIDS crisis (slow federal action under Reagan), the opioid epidemic (pharmaceutical industry oversight failures), COVID-19 (federal-state coordination breakdowns). Initial response is the key phrase — not the eventual outcome.
analyze

The HIV/AIDS crisis, the opioid epidemic, and COVID-19 all exposed gaps between public health needs and government response. What patterns do you see across these three crises?

How to approach this: Look for structural patterns, not just surface similarities — delayed response, politics overriding science, uneven impact across race and class. The strongest answers name the pattern precisely and give evidence from at least two of the three crises.
evaluate

Globalization, trade agreements, and immigration have all been described as both opportunities and threats to American workers. Using a specific example, argue which description is more accurate and why.

How to approach this: Pick a specific trade agreement (NAFTA) or industry (manufacturing in the Midwest) and trace its actual effects. Then take a position: on balance, did this specific example create more opportunity or more threat, and for whom?
