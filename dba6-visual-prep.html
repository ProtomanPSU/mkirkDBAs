Origins & Truman's Response

Quick contextYou are a diplomat in 1946. WWII just ended, and America and the Soviet Union are supposed to cooperate on rebuilding. But tension is rising. They want different things, trust each other less each day, and are arming themselves. A new kind of war is beginning.
understand

What were the Truman Doctrine, the Marshall Plan, and NATO, and how did each one try to contain Soviet influence?

How to approach this:
Define capitalism and communism. Explain why each nation saw the other's system as a threat.
analyze

Americans said the Soviets wanted to spread communism everywhere. Soviets said America was encircling them with military alliances. Which argument has more support from the evidence, or do both have merit?

How to approach this:
Why couldn't America and the Soviet Union compromise? What made their conflict so fundamental that it lasted 45 years?
evaluate

Could the U.S. and USSR have found a way to coexist peacefully after WWII, or was conflict structurally unavoidable given their ideologies and rival interests?

How to approach this:
Was the Cold War inevitable after WWII, or could smarter diplomacy have prevented it? Take a position.
EOC Practice Question

What made compromise between the U.S. and Soviet Union so difficult during the Cold War?

Crises, Korea & Vietnam

Quick contextYou work in the State Department during the Cold War. America's policy is to 'contain' communism: stop it from spreading anywhere, even in Korea, Vietnam. But these wars are costly. Is this policy wise?
understand

Describe one key event from the Korean War or the Cuban Missile Crisis that shows how close the Cold War came to becoming a direct military conflict between the U.S. and USSR.

How to approach this:
Explain what 'containment' meant and why policymakers believed in it. What was the 'domino theory'?
analyze

The U.S. had missiles in Turkey aimed at the USSR; the Soviets placed missiles in Cuba aimed at the U.S. Why did America treat these situations so differently? What does that reveal about perspective and threat perception?

How to approach this:
America fought in Korea and Vietnam but never directly fought the Soviet Union. Why? What prevented direct conflict?
create

You are advising the president during the Cuban Missile Crisis. Options include an air strike, a naval blockade, secret negotiations, or a public ultimatum. Each risks nuclear war in a different way. What do you recommend, and what are you willing to risk?

How to approach this:
Commit to a recommendation and explain what you gain, what you lose, and why the tradeoff is worth making.
EOC Practice Question

Why did the United States fight in Korea and Vietnam if it did not want direct war with the Soviet Union?

Correct Answer: B

Why this is correct: Containment policy meant opposing communist movements anywhere they arose, even in Korea and Vietnam. The U.S. believed that allowing one nation to fall to communism would encourage others.

Why the other answers are historically incorrect:
A: Economic interests played a role, but the primary motivation was anti-communism and the containment strategy.

C: These wars were directly related to Cold War competition; they were proxy conflicts between the superpowers.

D: The Soviet Union did not directly order America to fight; these were U.S. policy decisions based on containing communism.

The Red Scare & Life at Home

Quick contextYou are an American in the 1950s. Fear of communism is everywhere. Suspected communists are hunted. Schools teach anti-communist lessons. People fear Soviet spies. Is this fear justified, or are people being manipulated?
understand

Name one specific example of McCarthyism or Red Scare tactics and describe who was targeted and what happened to them.

How to approach this:
Explain what Mutually Assured Destruction (MAD) meant and why both sides accepted it.
analyze

McCarthyism, Hollywood blacklists, and espionage fears all shaped American life in the 1950s. Which had the most damaging effect on American democracy and why?

How to approach this:
How did the arms race actually create a kind of peace? What made nuclear weapons different from conventional ones?
evaluate

The Vietnam War eventually turned public opinion against the government. What role did the Pentagon Papers, Watergate, and the conduct of the war itself play in that shift? Which was most important?

How to approach this:
Was fighting proxy wars the right strategy? Would direct confrontation have been worse or better?
EOC Practice Question

How did the nuclear arms race paradoxically create a kind of peace during the Cold War?

Correct Answer: B

Why this is correct: The Cold War resulted from incompatible ideologies. The U.S. championed capitalism and democracy while the Soviet Union promoted communism and state control. Each side saw the other's system as a threat.

Why the other answers are historically incorrect:
A: The two nations had fundamentally different goals and ideologies; they did not want the same things.

C: The Soviet Union was a strong military power; the U.S. did not seek simple domination but rather to prevent Soviet expansion.

D: Ideological differences made compromise genuinely difficult, not just a matter of leaders' choices.
