Second Industrial Revolution

Quick context The Second Industrial Revolution transformed the U.S. after the Civil War through new technologies, railroads, and mass production. It created enormous wealth for some Americans while concentrating power in the hands of industrial giants like Carnegie, Rockefeller, and J.P. Morgan.
understand

Name two causes of the Second Industrial Revolution from different categories (transportation, communication, natural resources, entrepreneurs, technology, labor, or government). Why did these factors work together?

How to approach this: Pick two causes from genuinely different categories — don't just give two technology examples. Then explain the connection: how did each one make the other more powerful?
analyze

The Second Industrial Revolution affected the Northeast, Midwest, West, and South differently. Which region do you think changed most dramatically, and why?

How to approach this: Pick one region and commit to it. Your answer is stronger if you describe a specific industry or economic shift, not just general growth.
evaluate

How was the Second Industrial Revolution similar to and different from the First? Which difference matters most for understanding American history?

How to approach this: Don't list every similarity and difference — identify the one difference with the biggest consequences and explain why it changed what came next.

Labor, Immigration & Political Machines

Quick context Millions of immigrants flooded American cities to fill factory jobs, often working in dangerous conditions for low wages. Political machines like Tammany Hall traded basic services for immigrant votes, while industrialists used that same vulnerability to keep wages low and unions out.
analyze

Immigrants faced difficult conditions and were viewed with suspicion, yet they were also essential to industrial growth. How did American citizens and the government hold both of those things to be true at the same time?

How to approach this: Look for the tension: needed for labor, resented for culture. The strongest answer names a specific law, policy, or event that shows both attitudes existing simultaneously.
create

You work 12-hour days in a factory for low wages. You can keep your head down, join a labor union (risking your job and safety), or try to leave for something else. What would you do? What does your choice reveal about the options workers actually had?

How to approach this: Commit to a choice and defend it with the actual constraints workers faced — no savings, no English, family to feed. The most honest answers acknowledge why even a bad option might be the best available.

Reform & Social Change

Quick context The Progressive Era brought muckraking journalists, labor organizers, and government reformers who exposed and fought the worst abuses of industrialization. Changes like the Sherman Antitrust Act, the Pure Food and Drug Act, and the 17th Amendment came from sustained public pressure.
analyze

What had to happen before the government was willing to act on labor and social problems? Why was change so slow even when conditions were visibly terrible?

How to approach this: Think about what finally forced government action in specific cases — a disaster, a book, an election. Then explain the gap: why did terrible conditions exist for years before anything changed?
evaluate

The Populist Party, labor unions, and Progressive reformers all pushed for change in different ways. Which approach produced the most lasting results, and why?

How to approach this: Pick one and make the case for it. If you say Progressives, name a specific reform. If you say unions, name a specific victory. Specific examples always outperform general claims in a live conversation.
